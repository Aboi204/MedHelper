# Medical Lecture: Beta-Adrenergic Antagonists (Beta-Blockers)
Department of Pharmacology & Therapeutics

1. Overview:
Beta-adrenoceptor blocking agents are drugs that bind selectively to beta-receptors, preventing their activation by endogenous catecholamines (epinephrine and norepinephrine).

2. Receptor Subtypes:
- Beta-1 (Cardiac): Located predominantly on myocardium, SA node, AV node, and juxtaglomerular cells in the kidney.
  Activation leads to: increased heart rate (chronotropic), increased contractility (inotropic), increased AV conduction velocity (dromotropic), and increased renin secretion.
- Beta-2 (Smooth Muscle): Located on bronchial smooth muscle, vascular smooth muscle in skeletal muscles, liver, and uterus.
  Activation leads to: bronchodilation, vasodilation in skeletal muscle vascular beds, increased glycogenolysis, and uterine relaxation.
- Beta-3: Located in adipose tissue and urinary bladder detrusor muscle.

3. Classification of Beta-Blockers:
A. Non-selective (1st Generation): Block both Beta-1 and Beta-2.
   Examples: Propranolol, Timolol, Sotalol, Nadolol.
B. Cardioselective (2nd Generation): Predominantly block Beta-1 at therapeutic doses.
   Examples: Atenolol, Metoprolol, Bisoprolol, Esmolol.
C. Third Generation (With Vasodilating activity):
   - Alpha-1 blocking activity: Carvedilol, Labetalol.
   - Nitric oxide (NO) mediated vasodilation: Nebivolol.

4. Therapeutic Indications:
- Systemic Arterial Hypertension
- Ischemic Heart Disease (Angina Pectoris & Post-Myocardial Infarction)
- Chronic Stable Heart Failure (Bisoprolol, Carvedilol, Metoprolol succinate)
- Cardiac Arrhythmias (Supraventricular tachycardias)
- Non-cardiovascular: Migraine prophylaxis, Essential tremor, Performance anxiety, Glaucoma (Timolol eye drops).

5. Adverse Effects & Contraindications:
- Bradycardia, AV conduction block, acute decompensation in uncompensated heart failure.
- Bronchospasm in asthmatic and COPD patients.
- Cold extremities and Raynaud's phenomenon.
- Masking hypoglycemia symptoms (except diaphoresis/sweating) in diabetic patients.
- Sexual dysfunction and fatigue.
- Rebound hypertension and anginal attacks on abrupt withdrawal (due to receptor up-regulation).
